Pretraining, mid-training, and post-training are one design problem

  • Large models are now commonly trained in at least three data phases.
  • In Datology's work with Thomson Reuters, mid-training on legal plus general data used under 1% of the base model's pretraining tokens.
  • The same post-training recipe then gained almost three times as much from the mid-trained starting point.

Key terms

Pretraining
Training a model from scratch on a huge, broad dataset, usually by predicting the next token.
Mid-training
A middle stage that shifts the model toward a domain or skill with targeted data.
Post-training
Shaping a pretrained model's behavior with supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning.
Supervised fine-tuning (SFT)
Training on example inputs paired with good outputs.
Reinforcement learning (RL)
Training by trying actions and getting rewarded for good outcomes.
FLOPs
Floating-point operations: the standard way to count how much computation training or inference uses.
  • Six terms used throughout the deck
  • The three training stages
    • Pretraining builds broad knowledge, mid-training shifts focus, post-training shapes behavior
  • SFT versus RL
    • SFT copies good examples; RL learns from rewards on its own attempts
  • FLOPs
    • How talks compare compute budgets, for example "145 times less training compute"

Ghosts, not animals

LLMs are statistical simulations of human artifacts, shaped by pretraining, post-training, RL, and economics. Expect jagged, alien strengths, not a smooth human mind.

The right posture

Neither dismissal nor blind trust.

The human role

Own the understanding: specs, judgment, and taste.

Clean, curate, create, compose

Rephrase the best documents. Adapt without forgetting.

Rephrasing, not inventing

Turn high-quality documents into many formats, such as true-or-false questions. The facts come from the source, which he argues avoids model collapse. Choosing which documents to rephrase matters most.

Mid-training without forgetting

Keep most of the mix close to the pretraining distribution and add domain data on top. General skills held or improved in the legal example.

Repeat good data, up to a point

Repeating high-quality data beats adding low-quality data, up to a threshold he doesn't quantify.

Key terms

Data curation
Choosing, cleaning, and balancing what goes into a training set.
Deduplication
Removing exact or near-duplicate examples, which waste compute and can cause memorization.
Decontamination
Removing test-set content from training data so evaluations stay honest.
Synthetic data
Training data generated by models. Morcos's version rephrases real documents.
Model collapse
Degradation when models train on too much of their own invented output.
Mixture of experts (MoE)
A model with many expert subnetworks, only some active per token.

  • Synthetic data and model collapse

Try this: run a controlled data ablation

  1. Pick a small public text classification dataset and a simple model, like logistic regression.
  2. Train once on the raw data. Record held-out accuracy.
  3. Remove exact duplicates and very short examples. Train again with identical settings.
  4. Check the held-out set for examples that also appear in training. What changes if you remove them?

What you'll learn: Morcos's controlled comparison: change only the data, then measure.

  • A laptop-sized version of a data ablation
  • Step by step
    • Keep the model, features, and settings identical between runs
    • Only the data changes
    • Then do a simple decontamination check between train and test
  • What students should notice
    • Duplicates and overlap can inflate test scores
    • Small, careful changes are how you learn what data matters

Try this: write a reward, then try to hack it

  1. Pick a task with checkable answers, like arithmetic word problems.
  2. Write a reward function: 1 if the final number is correct, else 0.
  3. Write five fake outputs that would game it, such as listing many numbers.
  4. Patch the reward with a format check and a preference for the shortest correct answer.

Evaluation is now part of the training loop

The environments that score training are the same ones that score models. A flaw in the eval becomes a flaw in the model.

Leaks

Answers reach the model through paths nobody closed.

Noise

One run hides how much results vary across tasks and seeds.

Mismatch

The eval harness differs from how the model is really used.

Try this: put error bars on a benchmark

  1. Pick 50 questions with known answers in a subject you know.
  2. Run a model on them three times with sampling on. Record accuracy each time.
  3. Compute the mean and a simple bootstrap 95% interval.
  4. Compare two prompts. Does the difference survive the interval?

Predict representations, not pixels

21:47 Why generative models fail for video25:09 JEPA29:09 Training without collapse
  • On screen: the contrast at the heart of his talk
  • Generative models
    • Predict every detail of the future; for video, the unpredictable details make predictions blurry
  • JEPA, the joint embedding predictive architecture
    • Encode both input and target, then predict the target's representation
    • The encoder can drop details that can't be predicted
  • The hard part: collapse
    • If you only minimize prediction error, encoders can output constants
    • His preferred fix is maximizing information in the representation, for example regularizing it toward an isotropic Gaussian
  • He notes about 1,700 papers now mention JEPA

LeCun's advice to researchers

Joint embedding over generative

For world models and physical AI, abandon generative architectures.

Energy-based over probabilistic

Infer by searching for the output that minimizes an energy, then plan like model predictive control.

Minimize reinforcement learning

Learn by observation first. Use RL on top of good representations, when there is no alternative.

World models aren't video generators

Generating cute videos and understanding the world are different problems.

12:27 Energy-based inference40:54 Don't work on video generation56:28 Conclusions
  • His four recommendations, stated as his views
    • Joint embedding instead of generative architectures, if you care about intelligence for the physical world
    • Energy-based inference: search for actions that minimize an objective, with guardrail objectives that can't be jailbroken, in his framing
    • Minimize RL because of its sample inefficiency
    • Don't confuse video generation with world modeling
  • He also tells academics not to work on LLMs, saying they have little to add there
  • These are deliberately provocative; they sharply contrast with the RL-centric talks in topics 2 and 4

Early evidence, and the open problem

Learned common sense

V-JEPA's prediction error spikes on physically impossible videos, like a ball that vanishes.

Depth without labels

A head on V-JEPA 2.1 features predicts depth from a single image well, which he reads as learning the world is 3D.

Planning in latent space

World models trained on DINO features planned simple tasks within 25 steps.

Unsolved: hierarchical planning

He calls it completely open, and a great PhD topic.

Try this: watch a model forget

  1. Train a small network on MNIST digits 0 to 4. Record accuracy on them.
  2. Keep training it, now only on digits 5 to 9.
  3. Re-measure accuracy on 0 to 4. How much was lost?

  • On screen: the loop in Karpathy's autoresearch repository, from March 2026
  • How it works, per the README

What autoresearch teaches, and what to watch

The human role moves up

You write the research program. The agent writes the experiments.

Fixed budgets make comparisons fair

Every run gets the same wall-clock time, whatever the agent changes.

One metric invites gaming

An agent optimizing a single number can find changes that help the number, not the model.

Permissions and cost

Run with permissions disabled, on a disposable setup, and watch GPU and token spend.

  • Lessons beyond the repository
  • Encouraging
    • Research becomes writing good instructions and evaluation, which links back to Karpathy's verifiability argument
    • Fixed compute budgets are a simple, strong experimental design choice
  • Cautions, which are this deck's, not the README's
    • Goodhart's law: a single metric can be optimized in unhelpful ways
    • Agents with broad permissions on real infrastructure are risky; the README itself says to disable permissions

An experiment that changes one thing to see what it contributes.

Giving every experiment the same time or compute, so results are comparable.

Try this: be the autoresearch agent

Glossary: Ablation to Decontamination

Ablation
Changing one thing to see what it contributes.
Agentic engineering
Professional work with agents: specs, reviewed diffs, eval loops.
Autoresearch
Agents that run experiments and decide what to try next.
Benchmark
A fixed task set for comparing models.
Bits per byte
Bits needed per byte of text; lower is better.
Catastrophic forgetting
Losing old skills when training on new data.
Confidence interval
A range showing how much a result could vary by chance.
Contamination
Test answers leaking into training data or tools.
Continual learning
Learning from new experience after deployment.
Data curation
Choosing, cleaning, and balancing training data.
Decontamination
Removing test content from training data.

Glossary: Deduplication to Jaggedness

Deduplication
Removing exact or near-duplicate examples.
Diffusion model
Generates images or video by removing noise step by step.
Distillation
Training a small model to imitate a large one.
Energy-based model
Scores input–output compatibility; inference minimizes energy.
Environment
Task set, harness, and scoring rules for training or evaluation.
FLOPs
A count of computation.
Goodhart's law
A measure that becomes a target stops being a good measure.
Harness
The code that runs an agent: prompts, tools, loop.
Held-out set
Data kept out of training, used only for the final measurement.
Jaggedness
Uneven ability across similar tasks.

Glossary: JEPA to Reinforcement learning

JEPA
Predicts representations of missing data, not the data itself.
LLM-as-judge
A model grading outputs, often with a rubric.
Mid-training
A stage that shifts a model toward a domain with targeted data.
Mixture of experts
Many expert subnetworks, only some active per token.
Model collapse
Degradation from training on too much invented model output.
Model predictive control
Planning by simulating action sequences with a model.
Perceptual hash
An image fingerprint robust to small changes.
Post-training
SFT and RL that shape a pretrained model's behavior.
Pretraining
Training from scratch on huge, broad data.
Reinforcement learning
Learning from rewards on one's own attempts.

Glossary: Replay to World model

Replay
Mixing old data into new training to reduce forgetting.
Representation collapse
An encoder producing the same output for every input.
Reward hacking
Scoring well without doing what was intended.
Rollout
One complete recorded attempt at a task.
Rubric
A written scoring guide.
Self-supervised learning
Learning from the data itself, without labels.
Supervised fine-tuning
Training on inputs paired with good outputs.
Synthetic data
Training data generated by models.
Verifiability
How easily success can be checked automatically.
World model
A model that predicts how the world changes with actions.
